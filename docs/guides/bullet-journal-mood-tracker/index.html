Bullet journal mood tracker: 8 layouts that are easy to keep

A mood tracker is one of the most popular pages in any bullet journal, and one of the most often abandoned. These eight layouts range from two minute setups to small art projects. Pick one that fits how much time you really have.

By Caspar Baumeister. Updated 13 October 2026.

Why most mood trackers stall in February

It is rarely a lack of discipline. Usually the tracker is too much work for what it gives back. An elaborate drawing takes an hour to set up each month, and when you skip three days the page looks ruined. So the honest question before you pick a layout is: how many minutes per month do I really want to spend on this?

The good news: the simplest layouts are also the most useful ones. A plain grid with five colors shows patterns more clearly than a beautiful mandala.

Eight layouts, from simple to artsy

1. Year in Pixels

One square per day for the whole year on a single spread. Strongest at showing seasons and long streaks.

Effort to draw: Low. Effort per day: Medium.

2. Monthly calendar grid

A small calendar each month with every day colored in. Easy to start and fits next to your monthly log.

Effort to draw: Low. Effort per day: Low.

3. Mood circle

A circle cut into 28 to 31 slices, one per day, like a pie. Looks great, a bit fiddly to draw.

Effort to draw: Medium. Effort per day: Medium.

4. Line graph

One dot per day on a scale from low to high, connected into a line. Best for seeing ups and downs.

Effort to draw: Low. Effort per day: Low.

5. Fill in drawing

Leaves on a branch, books on a shelf, scales on a fish: one shape per day. Pretty, but drawing it takes time.

Effort to draw: High. Effort per day: High.

6. Weekly mood log

One line per day with a color dot and a few words. Good if you also want a short note.

Effort to draw: Low. Effort per day: Low.

7. Bar chart

Two thin bars per day, for example mood and energy, side by side. Shows relationships between two things.

Effort to draw: Medium. Effort per day: Low.

8. Mood and habit tracker

Rows of habits with a mood dot underneath each day. Lets you see whether habits and good days line up.

Effort to draw: Medium. Effort per day: Low.

Which one should I pick? If you are new, start with the monthly calendar grid or the line graph. If you love the idea of seeing a whole year on one page, go straight for the Year in Pixels template.

Set one up in 15 minutes

  1. Choose the question. Mood is the default. Write it at the top of the page so you answer the same question every day.
  2. Write the key first. Three to six moods with one color each. See the color key guide for palettes that stay readable.
  3. Draw the frame in pencil. Use the dot grid of your journal to keep boxes even. Ink it only when it looks right.
  4. Number the days. Small numbers save you from coloring the wrong box at 11 pm.
  5. Leave space for a monthly note. Two lines at the bottom for what stood out. In a year these notes are gold.

When you skip days

You will. Everyone does. Fill in the last two days from memory, leave older ones white and keep going. Do not tear out the page. A tracker with gaps still shows you more than no tracker, and the gaps themselves often tell a story: busy weeks, holidays, low phases.

If you skip a lot, make the daily step smaller. Move the tracker to the page you open every day anyway, or switch to a layout that takes ten seconds instead of a minute.

Questions and answers

What is the easiest bullet journal mood tracker?

A monthly calendar grid or a simple line graph. Both take a few minutes to set up and a few seconds per day.

How do I make a mood tracker in my bullet journal?

Choose a layout, write a key with three to six moods and colors, draw the frame in pencil, number the days and color one box per day. Fill in missed days within two days, otherwise leave them blank.

What should a mood tracker include?

The question, a color key with labels, one space per day and ideally a little room for a monthly note. You can add a second row for sleep or energy if you want to compare.

Should I use a monthly or a yearly mood tracker?

Monthly trackers are easier to start and can have a new theme each month. A yearly tracker shows long patterns and seasons much better. Many people do both: a monthly page for notes and a Year in Pixels for the big picture.
