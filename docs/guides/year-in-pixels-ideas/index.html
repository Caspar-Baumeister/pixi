Year in Pixels ideas: 36 things to track besides mood

Mood is the classic, but a Year in Pixels works for anything you can answer in one word at the end of a day. Here are 36 ideas, grouped by theme, each with a question and levels you can copy straight into your key.

How to choose what to track

Before you pick from the list, run your idea through three quick checks. If it passes all three, it will make a good grid.

  1. You can answer it in three seconds. If you need to look something up or think hard, you will skip days.
  2. It changes from day to day. A question with the same answer 300 times makes a boring picture.
  3. You are curious about the answer. The best grids answer a question you actually have, like whether you really sleep worse on Sundays.

Keep the levels short and concrete. Walk is easier to decide on than light activity. And give the lowest level a friendly name, so a lazy day does not feel like a red mark.

Feelings

The classic starting point. Feelings grids are the ones people look at most at the end of the year, because they show the shape of a year better than any calendar.

A temperature year

A temperature Year in Pixels is borrowed from the knitting world, where people knit one row of a blanket per day in the color of that day's high. On paper it is one of the most beautiful grids you can make, because it shows the seasons as a smooth wave of color from blue to red. Look up the high in your weather app each evening and color by bands of 10 degrees Celsius, or 15 to 20 degrees Fahrenheit. Pick a cool to warm palette such as dark blue, light blue, green, yellow, orange and red.

Tracking several things at once

You have three options when one question is not enough:

  • One page per question. The cleanest option. Lay the pages side by side at the end of a month and compare them.
  • Split the box. Divide each square diagonally and color one half per question. Works for two questions, not more.
  • Dots on top. Color the box for mood and add a small dot for a yes or no habit, like exercise.

In the Pixi app each question is its own map. You swipe between them, and Premium shows which colors from different maps tend to show up on the same days.

Ideas by person

  • Students: mood, sleep hours, study time, screen time.
  • Runners and athletes: training, energy, sleep quality, pain.
  • Cycle tracking: period, mood, energy, pain. Seeing them next to each other is often eye opening.
  • New parents: hours of sleep, time outside, time for yourself, mood.
  • Cutting back: alcohol, caffeine or screen time, together with mood and sleep.
  • Creative people: making things, reading, mood.

Once you have picked, the next step is the color key. Our guide to Year in Pixels colors has palettes for scales and for categories, and the free template has room for six levels.

Questions and answers

What is the best thing to track in a Year in Pixels?

Mood is the most popular choice because it changes daily and is easy to answer. Sleep quality is a close second and often the more useful one, because it explains many of the stormy days.

How many levels should my Year in Pixels have?

Three to six. Five works well for mood. Yes or no questions only need two or three levels, and categories like weather or dream types can have up to six.

Can I track two things in one Year in Pixels?

Yes, by splitting each square diagonally or adding a small dot for a second yes or no question. For more than two things, use one grid per question.

Is it too late to start a Year in Pixels in the middle of the year?

No. Start today and leave the earlier boxes empty, or start a fresh grid that runs 12 months from now. The picture is just as interesting.
